On 2 November 2026, the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) published a **draft amendment** to the Food Safety and Standards (Packaging) Regulations, 2018. It appeared in the Gazette of India on 3 November.

## What the draft proposes

- **Chemically recycled PE and PP for food contact.** Recycled polyethylene and polypropylene made through chemical recycling (the monomer route, as defined in IS 14534) could be used for food-contact materials, but only if the output is treated as equivalent to virgin material and meets virgin-material criteria.
- **Recycled polymer in the outer layer of multilayer packs.** Recycled polymer made only from food-grade packaging could go into the outermost layer of multilayer food packaging, as long as the inner layers act as a barrier and the pack meets existing migration limits.

## What happens next

The draft is open for objections and suggestions for **60 days** from the date the Gazette copies were made public. Comments go to the CEO, FSSAI, or by email to sp-packaging@fssai.gov.in.

## Our take

This is a draft, not final law. Mechanically recycled HDPE, which is what GreenCirkit makes, is still not cleared for direct food contact. But the direction is clear: regulators are building a path for recycled content in food packaging, starting where safety can be engineered in. For now, the biggest near-term demand for near-virgin rHDPE remains non-food rigid packaging (home care, personal care, lubricants, agro-chemicals), where the 40% recycled-content target for FY 2026–27 already applies.
